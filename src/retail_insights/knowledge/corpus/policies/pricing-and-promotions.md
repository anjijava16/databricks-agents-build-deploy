# Pricing and Promotions Playbook

## Principles

Cedarline Markets runs promotions to drive traffic and clear seasonal stock without training customers to wait for discounts. Every promotion must have an owner, a start and end date, a target category and a margin estimate signed off by the category finance partner.

## Approved discount levels

Line-level promotional discounts are limited to the following standard levels: 10%, 15% and 20%. Non-standard levels (for example 12% or 25%) are not allowed in the point-of-sale system.

## Maximum discount by category

The maximum promotional discount on a single order line depends on the category, because margins differ widely.

- Grocery: maximum 10% (average gross margin around 28%)
- Electronics: maximum 15% (average gross margin around 35%)
- Home: maximum 20% (average gross margin around 45%)
- Apparel: maximum 20% (average gross margin around 55%)
- Beauty: maximum 20% (average gross margin around 60%)

Any discount above the category maximum needs written approval from the VP of Merchandising before the promotion goes live. Lines sold above the category maximum without approval are treated as a pricing exception and reviewed in the monthly margin meeting.

## Promotion frequency

A product should not be on promotion for more than 12 weeks in any rolling 52-week period. Electronics promotions are limited to two windows per year outside the holiday season.

## Channel rules

Promotions apply equally in stores and online unless the promotion is explicitly marked as online-only. Online-only promotions may not exceed the category maximum either.

## Measuring promotions

Promotion performance is measured with net revenue, units, gross margin and average order value from the gold sales tables. A promotion is considered successful when incremental gross margin over the promotion window is positive compared with the same weeks of the prior period. Discounts are reported in the discounts column of the daily sales table, and net revenue is always after discounts.

## Price matching

Stores match the advertised price of a local competitor on identical items in stock, excluding marketplace sellers, clearance and membership-only prices. Price matching is not available on Grocery.
